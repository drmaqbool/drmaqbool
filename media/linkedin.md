## World Heart Day 2026

**Posted:** 30 September 2026 (12:44 UTC) · [Original LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mohd-maqbool-sohil-5261a49a_this-world-heart-day-29-september-2026-activity-7511043300162691073-yFLF)

![World Heart Day 2026: Saving Hearts, Restoring Hope](science/2026-09-30-world-heart-day-2026-dont-miss-a-beat.jpg)

> \*\*\*This World Heart Day (29 September 2026), as an interventional cardiologist I reflect on how far we’ve come — and what we must do next to reduce cardiovascular mortality and disability worldwide.
>
> Cardiovascular disease remains the leading cause of death and disability globally, but the past decade brought important advances: precision imaging, drug‑eluting and bioresorbable scaffold technologies, radial‑first access, hybrid procedures, optimized antithrombotic regimens, and AI‑assisted risk stratification. These improvements translate into fewer peri‑procedural complications, shorter hospital stays, and better long‑term outcomes. Yet disparities persist: low‑ and middle‑income regions face gaps in primary prevention, timely diagnosis, and access to life‑saving interventions.
>
> Three priorities for 2026 and beyond  
> - Strengthen primary prevention. Control of hypertension, tobacco cessation, diabetes management, and dyslipidemia remain the most effective ways to cut population risk. Scalable community programs and task‑sharing models are essential in resource-limited settings.  
> - Improve access and systems of care. Time is myocardium. Expand primary PCI networks, organized pre‑hospital triage, and telecardiology support so patients reach definitive care rapidly. Invest in training and retention of cath lab teams in underserved regions.  
> - Personalize and de‑risk interventional care. Use multimodality imaging (CT, IVUS, OCT), physiology (FFR, iFR), and population‑specific antithrombotic algorithms to tailor treatments and minimize bleeding and ischemic complications. Integrate AI tools to enhance triage and procedural planning while rigorously validating them across diverse populations.
>
> Clinical reminder for colleagues  
> - Adopt radial access as default when feasible to lower bleeding and mortality.  
> - Use evidence‑based secondary prevention: high‑intensity statin therapy, SGLT2 inhibitors or GLP‑1 receptor agonists where indicated, strict BP control, and structured cardiac rehabilitation.  
> - For STEMI and high‑risk ACS, target door‑to‑device times per guideline, but also prioritize systems that reduce pre‑hospital delay.
>
> Call to action  
> - Clinicians: advocate for prevention programs, train the next generation in both technical skills and systems thinking, and publish real‑world data from diverse populations.  
> - Health systems: prioritize funding for primary care, emergency transport, and cath lab infrastructure.  
> - Community: promote heart‑healthy lifestyles, hypertension screening, and rapid response to chest pain.
>
> On World Heart Day 2026, let’s commit to equitable implementation of proven strategies — prevention first, faster access second, precision interventional care third — so that every heart, everywhere, has a fighting chance.

## Complex multi-vessel PCI: RCA CTO and LAD/diagonal bifurcation

**Posted:** 29 November 2025 (05:26 UTC) · [Original LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mohd-maqbool-sohil-5261a49a_just-wrapped-up-a-highly-complex-and-rewarding-activity-7400404649524178944-_R3v)

Video: [PCI of RCA CTO and LAD/diagonal bifurcation](procedures/2025-11-29-pci-rca-cto-and-lad-diagonal-bifurcation.mp4) (MP4, 11 MB, no audio)

> Just wrapped up a highly complex and rewarding PCI case involving multi-vessel disease with a combination of difficult lesions. The patient presented with a Right Coronary Artery (RCA) Chronic Total Occlusion (CTO) and a critical  Left Anterior Descending (LAD) + Diagonal bifurcation lesion.  
> This is precisely where the art and science of interventional cardiology merge! 🛠️  
> Procedural Insights:  
> RCA CTO: Tackled via a successful anterograde by using 2-Pilot 200 wires and 1x5 mm CTO balloon
>
> LAD/Diagonal Bifurcation: A true bifurcation lesion that required a dedicated two-stent strategy (Mini-Crush technique) to secure both the main branch and the significant side branch, preventing side branch occlusion.  
> Intravascular Imaging (IVI): Meticulous use of IVI (IVUS) was critical throughout the procedure to confirm wire position, optimize stent deployment, and ensure an optimal  result.  
> Key Takeaways:  
> Complexity is Common: CTOs  in combination  with bifurcations are a significant challenge and require advanced techniques and careful planning.  
> Strategy is Everything: A hybrid approach and having a plan B (and C!) are crucial for success in these scenarios.
